False friends in Portuguese: 8 words English speakers get wrong

Na Ponta da Língua · 5 min read

A false friend is a word that looks like an English word and means something else. Portuguese has plenty, and they are not obscure: they appear in shops, in emails and in the middle of a normal conversation, usually at the worst possible moment.

These eight are the ones English speakers get wrong most often. Each one comes with the word that actually means what you were trying to say, so you leave with both halves.

1. actually → na verdade

The trap: not «actualmente», which means *currently*.

Say: «Na verdade, não estou cansada.» Actually, I am not tired.

It sounds like a small thing. It is the difference between saying what you mean and telling someone what is happening right now.

2. preservative → conservante

The trap: not «preservativo», which means *condom*.

Say: «Este alimento tem conservantes.» This food has preservatives.

This is the most expensive of the eight, and the one people remember forever after the first mistake.

3. pretend → fingir

The trap: not «pretende», which means *intends*.

Say: «Ela finge não me ver.» She pretends not to see me.

The Portuguese word for what someone intends is pretende, so the confusion runs both directions in the same conversation.

4. parents → pais

The trap: not «parentes», which means *relatives*.

Say: «Os meus pais são muito simpáticos.» My parents are very nice.

Parentes exist too, and they are the wider family. Pais are the two people who raised you.

5. lunch → almoço

The trap: not «lanche», which means *snack*.

Say: «Hoje o almoço é às 13h.» Lunch is at 1 pm today.

Lanche is the small meal between meals, and in Portugal it is a real thing, taken mid afternoon. If you ask for lunch and say lanche, you will be handed a coffee and a pastry, and nobody will think it strange.

6. costume → disfarce

The trap: not «costume», which means *habit*.

Say: «É um disfarce muito original.» It is a very original costume.

Costume in Portuguese is what you usually do, or a traditional dress. For a party outfit, disfarce is the word.

7. vulgar → grosseiro

The trap: not «vulgar», which means *common* or *ordinary*.

Say: «Esse comportamento é grosseiro.» That behaviour is rude.

In Portuguese, vulgar is neutral. Saying that something is vulgar is not an insult, it is a description of how ordinary it is.

8. constipation → obstipação

The trap: not «constipação», which means *a cold*.

Say: «Tenho obstipação desde ontem.» I have been constipated since yesterday.

One word for a blocked nose and another for a blocked intestine, and the wrong one turns a pharmacy visit into a story you tell afterwards.

One that is not a false friend

«Really» is often taught alongside these, but it does not belong here. «Realmente» exists in Portuguese and means really or truly. What changes is the intensifier: «mesmo» strengthens what follows.

«Isso é mesmo interessante» means that is really interesting. It is a difference of usage, not a trap of meaning, and treating it as one would teach a rule that does not exist.

How to remember them

Do not learn the eight as a list. Take one a week and use it in a sentence about yourself, out loud. The mistake only becomes impossible after you have made it once and been corrected, and Portuguese speakers correct these with a smile, because they know exactly how the sentence was built.
