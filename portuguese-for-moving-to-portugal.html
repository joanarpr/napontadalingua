Portuguese for Moving to Portugal: What You Actually Need

Na Ponta da Língua · 6 min read

Moving to Portugal usually comes with a long to-do list, and the language rarely sits at the top of it. It should. Not because you have to be fluent before you land, but because a little Portuguese changes how every other item on that list goes: the bank, the doctor, the school, the neighbour who holds the gate.

This page is the practical version. What you need before you arrive, what you need in the first months, and what you need when you are ready to stay for good.

Before you move

You do not need to be fluent. You need enough to be polite and to read the essentials.

The list that matters most is short: greet people, introduce yourself, order, ask for directions, understand numbers and prices. That is about two or three months of regular lessons, not a degree.

If you are coming from French, Spanish or Italian, the structure of Portuguese will feel familiar and the work goes faster. If you are coming from English, the accent and the verb endings are the part that needs the most attention, and it is the part that makes people feel stuck early.

The first months, the Portuguese you hear daily

Textbooks teach the language you are supposed to hear. Portugal gives you the one people actually speak.

A few everyday things to expect: people speak fast and soften the ends of words; the greetings change with the time of day and the level of formality; and the polite form is the third person, not a special word. You do not need to master this on day one. You need to know it exists, so you do not think you are doing it wrong.

This is also when the vocabulary of real life shows up: the café, the pharmacy, the supermarket, the mechanic. These are not grammar. They are the difference between managing your day and surviving it.

When you want to stay: the A2 level

If your plan is to stay for good, there is a formal goal that turns language learning from optional to necessary: Portuguese nationality.

To apply for Portuguese nationality, you need to show you can use the language at A2 level, and the recognised way to do that is the CIPLE exam. It tests reading, writing, listening and speaking, and it is a real, structured target, not an abstract promise of «one day».

We prepare students for the CIPLE with a clear plan: the level, the exam format, and enough practice that the exam feels like another lesson rather than a judgement.

How lessons fit the move

Different moments need different things, so the lessons should not be the same at every stage:

  1. Before the move: survival Portuguese, twice a week, with the practical vocabulary of arrival.
  2. First months: conversation practice and the everyday situations, so the language stops being a barrier.
  3. Staying for good: a structured path to A2 and the CIPLE exam.
